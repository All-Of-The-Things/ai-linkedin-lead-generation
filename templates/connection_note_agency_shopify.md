## Style guide

- Open with "Hi {{first_name}}." — period after the name, never a comma, never "Hey"
- Lead with a **problem, gap, or outcome** relevant to their Shopify practice — NOT with who we are or what we run
- **"We" and "My Team"** (capital T) are both default pronouns — mix them across notes. Never use the same pronoun in every message in a batch.
- Two angles to choose from based on the lead's profile:
  - **Overflow angle**: when their Shopify Plus pipeline gets busy, we embed as extra build capacity — they keep the client relationship
  - **SuiteCommerce cross-sell angle**: for Shopify agencies whose clients also run NetSuite or ask about SuiteCommerce — we bring that capability they don't carry in-house
  - Read the headline carefully. If the company is purely Shopify-focused with no NetSuite/ERP signals, use the overflow angle. If there are ERP/NetSuite signals alongside Shopify, use the cross-sell angle.
- **Vary the partnership vocabulary** — see the vocabulary bank below. Never use the same expression twice in a batch
- **Never use "hire" language for agency contacts.** "Without growing headcount" is acceptable.
- Em-dashes are allowed as a structural separator — one per note maximum
- End with a **specific, low-friction meeting ask** — rotate these: "Available to chat this week?", "Are you open to a quick call?", "Worth a quick call?", "Can we explore a fit?", "Open to a quick call?", "Available to explore?"
- Hard limit: 300 characters

## Partnership vocabulary bank (vary — never repeat the same expression in a batch)

**Relationship framing**: "your delivery arm", "under your banner", "an extension of your Team", "as your build partner", "bolt-on build capacity", "your dedicated overflow squad", "we embed when you need the extra hands"

**Capacity framing**: "absorb that overflow", "bolt-on capacity when the scope calls for it", "on demand Shopify Plus expertise", "more output, same team size on your end", "you keep the account, we supply the build muscle", "augment your Team when things get busy"

**Service offering framing**: "enable that service line", "expand your offering into SuiteCommerce", "add that capability without growing your Team", "My Team handles the SuiteCommerce side when clients ask"

**Outcome framing**: "your client gets a clean delivery", "the client never feels the gap", "you keep the relationship, we handle the build", "single-vendor experience for your clients"

## Example messages (voice reference — do not copy verbatim)

"Hi {{first_name}}. Shopify Plus agencies often hit capacity walls on larger builds. We embed as the overflow squad — you keep the client relationship, we handle the build under your banner. Available to explore?"

"Hi {{first_name}}. When {{current_company}}'s Shopify Plus pipeline backs up, My Team can absorb that overflow — implementations, custom builds, integrations. You stay on the account, we handle the execution. Worth a quick call?"

"Hi {{first_name}}. We work with Shopify agencies as their SuiteCommerce build arm when clients ask about NetSuite storefronts. My Team handles that layer so you can say yes to that scope. Are you open to a quick call?"

"Hi {{first_name}}. Shopify Plus agencies we work with use us for SuiteCommerce scope when clients run NetSuite. Enables a wider offer without adding to your Team. Can we explore a fit?"

## What to personalise

- Read their headline: if they mention NetSuite, Oracle, or ERP, use the SuiteCommerce cross-sell angle; otherwise use the overflow angle
- If the company is Aztech Digital or another known Shopify Plus agency, be specific about what their clients encounter
- If their role is more technical (Solutions Architect, Practice Lead), lean into the capability angle
- If their role is more executive (CEO, MD, CRO), lean into the business/revenue angle — "more output", "wider offer", "keep the account"
- **For direct client contacts** (a CTO or in-house operator at a brand, not an agency): drop the partner framing. Frame as: "we take on that build layer so your team doesn't have to"
